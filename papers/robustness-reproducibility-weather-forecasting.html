TL;DR

Data-driven weather models are already used next to physics-based ones. Physics-based models could be reproduced exactly and their parameters had a physical meaning; machine-learning models give up both. This white paper walks through the AI lifecycle (data, training, deployment) and shows where robustness and reproducibility problems arise, how to mitigate them, and what is still open.

What the paper covers

Robustness is a core requirement for trustworthy AI, and the EU AI Act makes it an explicit obligation for high-risk systems. Weather models are not classed as high-risk, but the same requirements are a useful guide for voluntary codes of conduct. The paper takes the AI lifecycle as its structure and asks, at each stage, what can go wrong and what helps.

  • Robustness along the AI lifecycle. Terminology, uncertainty quantification, and the stages where problems enter.
  • Data acquisition and preparation. Collection, curation and validation, and transformation of data.
  • Training and model building. Challenges in the training process and possible mitigations.
  • Deployment and operation. Validation and quality gates, integration into existing software and hardware, and performance degradation over time.
  • Governance. A process-oriented view, the role of international standardization, and a scientific perspective on reproducibility.

One point stressed throughout is that uncertainty in data-driven forecasts has a new source on top of the chaotic dynamics of the atmosphere and limited sensor data: the machine-learning model itself. The paper also notes that AI helps with the problem it creates, for example through cheaper approximations of uncertainty distributions.
