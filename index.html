sadeghi@afshn.com Google Scholar LinkedIn

Large language models are fluent but not reliable: they state falsehoods confidently, drift from their evidence, and are hard to steer toward honest behavior. My research is about closing that gap, making the outputs of LLMs faithful, verifiable, and aligned with what a model actually knows.

I focus on this from three directions: detecting hallucinations before they reach the user, intervening on a model's internal representations at inference time to shape its behavior, and grounding generation in retrieved evidence so that answers can be checked. Earlier in my career I worked on representation learning and knowledge-graph embeddings, which still shapes how I think about structure and reliability in modern AI systems.

I hold a PhD in Artificial Intelligence from the University of Bonn, and have so far authored 15+ peer-reviewed publications, including first-author work at NeurIPS. Since Last year I joined AI Assurance and Assessment department to more focus on my research of AI safety.
