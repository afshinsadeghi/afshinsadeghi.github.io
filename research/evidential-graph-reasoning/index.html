Evidential Graph Reasoning for LLM Factuality:

A Belief-Function Fusion of Retrieved Triples

In easy words

A fact checker built on an LLM is a black box: you hand it the evidence, it hands back “true” or “false”, and you cannot see how any single fact counted. We replace that step with arithmetic. Each retrieved fact becomes a small piece of evidence for or against the claim on a graph, the pieces are combined with a graph belief function based on Dempster–Shafer theory, and when ignorance ends up with the largest share, the symbolic layer says “not enough information” and the full system asks the plain LLM instead of guessing.

On the claim-focused Answer benchmark, where the evidence is a short passage written for the claim, the reported accuracy is higher than GPT-4.1 on its own, retrieval (RAG) and GraphRAG. On noisy or misleading evidence the outcome depends on the model: with Gemma the accuracy still improves on every benchmark, while with GPT-4.1 and Llama there is little or no advantage, and by some metrics it is worse, clearly so for Llama on Gold. Two label-free statistics of the retrieved evidence show in advance whether it carries any direction at all, though not whether that direction is right.

TL;DR

Retrieval-augmented fact checking usually hands the retrieved evidence to an LLM and takes whatever verdict comes back. How each fact influenced the verdict is not represented, and a verdict is returned even when the evidence is weak or conflicting.

We turn the evidence into a knowledge graph of triples, give each triple a graded support or refutation mass, combine the masses with an explicit Dempster–Shafer calculation, and abstain (defer to the no-retrieval LLM) when the combined mass supports neither verdict.

The result is selective, and the paper says so. With GPT-4.1, the full system has higher accuracy than raw-text RAG and adapted GraphRAG on all four benchmarks, and higher accuracy than the no-retrieval LLM on three. That advantage depends on the retrieved triples carrying directional signal: when they are mostly neutral, or point the wrong way, it disappears.

1A verdict nobody can audit

In claim verification, a system reads a claim and some evidence and returns one of three outcomes: confirmed, refuted, or not enough information (NEI).

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) supplies the evidence as text. Graph-RAG supplies it as a retrieved subgraph of facts. In both, the facts are then passed back to an LLM that reasons over them in one generation step. Two things follow from that design.

  • The influence of each individual fact on the verdict is not represented anywhere. You get a label and perhaps an explanation, but not a computation you can inspect.
  • LLMs tend to overcommit under uncertainty rather than abstain. In our setting, an LLM judge reading the retrieved triples as text commits on 100% of claims (its prompt offers an insufficient-evidence label, which it did not use in Table 4), including the ones where the triples carry no usable signal.

LLM-as-judge over retrieved evidence

claim + triples (as text) → LLM → verdict
  • Each fact's contribution is hidden inside the generation.
  • Weak or conflicting evidence still gets a verdict.
  • No separate channel for “I do not know”.

Evidential Graph Reasoning (this paper)

claim + triples → per-triple masses → fusion → verdict or abstain
  • Each triple carries an explicit support / refute / ignorance mass.
  • Fusion is arithmetic you can trace back to single triples.
  • When ignorance has the largest fused mass, the symbolic layer abstains, and the final answer falls back to the no-retrieval LLM.

The rest of this page walks through the method and lets you operate it. Everything labelled illustrative is a toy scenario made for this page. Everything labelled with a table number comes from the paper.

2The pipeline, step by step

The pipeline, laid out as in the paper's figure: documents and the user's claim are turned into a knowledge graph, the claim-related triples are retrieved and minimised to Steiner trees, each surviving triple is scored for direction and directness (E) and strength (W), the scores become belief masses at triple and subgraph level, and the graph belief function returns true, false or “not enough information”.

Walkthrough

3From a triple to a belief mass

Each retrieved triple \(i\) is scored against the claim on two scales.

  • Polarity \(E_i\in\{-2,-1,0,+1,+2\}\): \(+2\) direct support, \(+1\) indirect support, \(0\) no directional evidence, \(-1\) indirect refutation, \(-2\) direct refutation.
  • Strength \(W_i\in[0,1]\): how strongly the triple supports or refutes, whether that is direct or indirect. It is a property of the evidential relation, not the LLM's confidence in its own label.

The frame of discernment is \(\Theta=\{C,R\}\) (Confirmed, Refuted). A triple becomes a basic belief assignment over \(\{C\}\), \(\{R\}\) and \(\Theta\) (ignorance):

\[ m_i(\{C\})=\Big(\tfrac{\max(E_i,0)}{2}\Big)^{2}W_i,\qquad m_i(\{R\})=\Big(\tfrac{\max(-E_i,0)}{2}\Big)^{2}W_i,\qquad m_i(\Theta)=1-m_i(\{C\})-m_i(\{R\}). \]

Squaring the normalised amplitude makes indirect evidence four times weaker than direct evidence at the same strength. A neutral triple (\(E_i=0\)) puts all its mass on ignorance. It is the identity element of the flat combination: adding it leaves the fused result unchanged. Neutral triples can still take part in an informative compound (Section 5).

4Fusing evidence, and what happens to conflict

With one mass per triple, the next job is to combine them. The paper uses Yager's rule. Where Dempster's rule normalises conflicting mass away, Yager's rule moves it to ignorance \(\Theta\). Dempster's normalisation can amplify a thin slice of agreement under high conflict; Yager's rule avoids that, at a cost discussed in Section 8.

The rule is applied in n-ary batch form: take three products over all triples at once, then reallocate the conflict once at the root.

\[ s_C=\prod_i\big(m_i(\{C\})+m_i(\Theta)\big),\quad s_R=\prod_i\big(m_i(\{R\})+m_i(\Theta)\big),\quad s_\Theta=\prod_i m_i(\Theta), \] \[ K=1-s_C-s_R+s_\Theta,\qquad m(\{C\})=s_C-s_\Theta,\quad m(\{R\})=s_R-s_\Theta,\quad m(\Theta)=s_\Theta+K. \]

The verdict is \(\hat y=\arg\max\big(m(\{C\}),m(\{R\}),m(\Theta)\big)\). When \(\Theta\) wins, the evidence is either too weak to commit or mutually contradictory, and no tuned threshold is involved. Because it is a single product over all sources, the result does not depend on the order of the triples.

5Compound evidence: the hierarchical variant

Flat fusion treats every triple as an independent source of equal standing. Knowledge graphs also contain patterns that a single product cannot represent. H-GBF adds a layer of compound nodes between triples and the root. An LLM group identifier proposes groups of triples with a type and a confidence \(\gamma_\varphi\); a type-specific operator then computes a bound \(\tilde m_\varphi\) from the children's masses.

Here \(\mu=\min_i\big(m_i(C)+m_i(R)\big)\), \(a=\sigma\big(2(\sum_i m_i(C)-\tau_a)\big)\), \(\bar r=\tfrac1l\sum_i m_i(R)\), and \(\kappa\) is a proxy for coverage computed from the retrieved subgraph (the normalised count of refuting-polarity triples), with no extra model call; it is not a measure of completeness. In each case \(\tilde m_\varphi(\Theta)=1-\tilde m_\varphi(C)-\tilde m_\varphi(R)\), with the polar terms rescaled if they sum above 1.

For every type except chains, the compound's final mass mixes the operator bound with a direct LLM estimate, with a weight that depends on the confidence and on how undecided the bound is, and is then discounted by \(\gamma_\varphi\):

\[ \lambda_\varphi=\gamma_\varphi\,\tilde m_\varphi(\Theta),\qquad m^{\mathrm{raw}}_\varphi(A)=\lambda_\varphi\,m^{\mathrm{LLM}}_\varphi(A)+(1-\lambda_\varphi)\,\tilde m_\varphi(A), \] \[ m_\varphi(A)=\gamma_\varphi\,m^{\mathrm{raw}}_\varphi(A),\qquad A\in\{C,R\}. \]

So \(\gamma_\varphi\) enters twice, in the mixing weight and in the final discount, and a low-confidence grouping contributes mostly ignorance to the root. Chains skip the mixture and enforce the operator bound, so a direct LLM estimate cannot override a broken link. The root fuses all compound and orphan-leaf masses with the same Yager rule; a flat pipeline is the special case with no compounds. The picture of one layer between triples and the root is a simplification: the paper defines a DAG in which children can be shared and a compound can itself be a child.

6Results

We evaluate on AVeriTeC (three versions of its evidence collection, treated as three benchmarks of increasing difficulty) and PubHealth. Answer is a short passage addressing the claim. Gold is a related document with noisy and sometimes conflicting content. Docs is four background documents per claim, truncated at 12,000 characters each. PubHealth has one resource document per claim. All systems are scored on the same surviving claims within each benchmark.

Three baselines all let an LLM resolve the final verdict, so the comparison is like for like on the resolution mechanism. Vanilla-LLM is GPT-4.1 with no retrieval. RAG retrieves raw text passages. GraphRAG (adapted-summary) summarises the same Steiner-pruned subgraph with an LLM. H-GBF (full) is our hierarchical fusion plus deferral to the Vanilla-LLM prior on abstention.

How does each method score?

Reading the table: the pattern is mixed and tracks retrieval quality rather than method.

  • H-GBF (full) has the highest accuracy on Gold, Answer and PubHealth, exceeding the no-retrieval baseline by 0.85, 3.56 and 0.68 points. On Answer it also has the best macro-F1.
  • On Docs it falls just below Vanilla-LLM on both metrics (−0.48 accuracy).
  • On PubHealth macro-F1 it is below Vanilla-LLM (54.03 against 56.45), and raw-text RAG has the best macro-F1 there (59.51), where the class distribution is most skewed.
  • RAG loses accuracy to the parametric prior on every benchmark. GraphRAG is weakest throughout and degrades sharply on Docs (24.11% accuracy), where the retrieved subgraph is largest and least directional.

7When fusion helps, and when it does not

The central finding is what the paper calls a retrieval-binding boundary. Symbolic fusion improves accuracy over a strong no-retrieval baseline only when the retrieved triples carry directional signal. Two properties of the retrieved evidence, shown in Table 2, bound what any fusion method can recover.

  • Neutral-triple rate: the share of matched triples with \(E_i=0\). These add no direction. It is observable without labels.
  • Misleading claims: the share of claims whose majority retrieved polarity opposes the gold label (for CONF and REJ), or is non-neutral when the label is NEI. This needs labels, so it is only a retrospective diagnostic.

Two different ways to be unhelpful

Misleading evidence (PubHealth). H-GBF slightly improves accuracy over Vanilla-LLM (67.75 against 67.07) but loses macro-F1. Both systems commit polar verdicts, by different routes. Vanilla-LLM can override the face-value polarity of retrieved facts using what it already knows. H-GBF encodes that polarity into the masses and commits by argmax over the fused result. Nearly half of refuted PubHealth claims (49.7%) have a majority of retrieved evidence that supports them, so the fusion commits mass on wrong-signed evidence. Deferral cannot repair this, because it fires only when the fusion abstains.

Absent signal (Docs). Here the evidence is dominated by neutral triples (91.6% of matched triples), although misleading polar evidence also occurs (33.5% of refuted Docs claims). Deferring to the parametric prior is the right response for the neutral part, and H-GBF (full) defers on the remaining 44.4% of claims. Standalone H-GBF still reaches 70.98% committed accuracy at a 55.6% commit rate, so the hierarchy does find a subset where it can commit with high precision. The full system gains little, because the remaining claims mostly defer to an already strong prior. The paper's reading is that the limitation on Docs is insufficient directional coverage, not low committed precision.

8Limitations the paper states

  • Yager's rule conflates conflict with ignorance. The deployed decision rule cannot tell an absence of evidence from strong contradictory evidence using the root masses alone. Recomputing the pre-redistribution conflict \(K\) per claim shows this is not negligible: on PubHealth it averages 0.21 overall and 0.72 on the disagreement subset (claims with at least two positively and two negatively scored triples), of which 79.6% resolve to abstention. An unnormalised formulation (the third rule in the playground) separates the two states measurably on PubHealth (Cohen's d = 0.42) but not on the other three benchmarks, and no decision rule built on that separation improved macro-F1 within confidence intervals.
  • The masses are not calibrated. They are built from language-model polarity scores that are not checked against observed reliability, so a mass of 0.8 does not mean the triple supports the claim in 80% of such cases. Calibrating them would need a model trained specifically for triple-level evidential scoring.
  • Independence is assumed. The product form treats sources as distinct bodies of evidence, which retrieved evidence does not always satisfy. Duplicated triples can inflate support. Cosine discounting and near-duplicate clustering did not help; limiting the number of fused sources with per-polarity root filtering did.
  • Performance is bounded by the group identifier. The gains transfer to Gemma but not to Llama-3.3-70B, which rarely emits compounds.
  • The advantage is selective across benchmarks, models and metrics, as the results above show.
  • Inspectability is conditional. Explicit arithmetic makes the aggregation traceable, but the extracted facts, their polarity scores and the compound groupings come from an LLM and are not validated by the fusion.

What this paper adds

  • The symbolic verdict is computed by explicit fusion. The decompositional, graph-based RAG and path-based methods discussed in the paper surface which facts or subgraph support an answer, then hand the combination back to an LLM. Here each retrieved triple becomes a basic belief assignment over {confirmed, refuted, ignorance}, compositional graph operators aggregate them over a hierarchy, and a verdict or abstention rule reads the fused mass. A committed verdict can be traced back to the triples that produced it; in the full system an abstention is resolved by the no-retrieval LLM, so only the symbolic part is computed.
  • “Not enough information” falls out of the calculus. Ignorance is an explicit mass that commits to neither side, so the NEI outcome comes from the fusion itself rather than from a tuned confidence threshold, and abstention is a visible state instead of something hidden in a generation step. When the system abstains it defers to the no-retrieval LLM.
  • On the same triples, fusion is more accurate on the claims it commits to, while abstaining on the others. With retrieval held fixed and no LLM fallback, committed accuracy is higher than that of the LLM judge, which answers every claim, on Gold, Answer and Docs, and the hierarchy raises the Docs commit rate from 32.9% to 55.6% while committed accuracy rises from 55.10 to 70.98.
  • Higher reported accuracy than raw-text RAG and adapted GraphRAG in all four GPT-4.1 settings, and highest accuracy of all methods on Gold, Answer and PubHealth (+0.85, +3.56 and +0.68 points over the no-retrieval baseline). The gains over each model's own no-retrieval baseline transfer to Gemma on all four benchmarks (point estimates; no significance tests are reported).
  • A retrieval-binding boundary, with a label-free screen. Symbolic fusion helps when retrieved triples carry directional signal and stops helping when they are mostly neutral or frequently wrong-signed. Neutral-triple rate and polar coverage can be read without labels and show in advance whether the retrieved evidence carries any direction at all; they are a diagnostic, not a guarantee of a gain, and wrong-signed evidence can only be diagnosed afterwards on labelled data.

Combining evidence with belief functions makes a fact check auditable and gives abstention a principled home, but it cannot repair evidence that points the wrong way.
